Make it simple

Short and simple is perfect

Your person may give a long answer, in English or in Italian. You do not need to write all of it. You turn it into short Italian sentences. One idea = one sentence.

The 3 steps

  1. Find the key fact. Who? What? Where? When? Pick the one that answers your question.
  2. Use the answer starter. Your report page gives you one for each question. Fill in the blank.
  3. Cut the extras. Leave out stories, "well…", and small details. If there are two facts, write two short sentences.

See how it works

They say: "Well, when I was little we lived in a small house near the sea in Sorrento, and my whole family lived there."
You write: Abitava a Sorrento. La casa era piccola. La casa era vicino al mare.
They say: "I went to school on foot every morning, about twenty minutes, with my best friend."
You write: Andava a scuola a piedi. Andava a scuola con la sua migliore amica.
They say: "Every Sunday my mother made pasta and we all ate together in the kitchen."
You write: La domenica sua madre faceva la pasta. La famiglia mangiava in cucina.
They say: "I started working at sixteen in a shoe store. My uncle helped me get the job."
You write: Ha cominciato a lavorare a sedici anni. Il suo primo lavoro è stato in un negozio di scarpe.
The uncle is a small detail, so it is cut.
They say: "In 1970 I took the train for the first time and went to Rome to see my cousin."
You write: Nel 1970 ha preso il treno per la prima volta. È andata a Roma.

Notice: a habit or a description uses the imperfetto (abitava, era, faceva). One finished event uses the passato prossimo (ha cominciato, è andata).

Say this to your person

Tell them you need short answers. You can say it in English or in Italian.

English: "Short answers are perfect. One word or one short sentence."
Italiano: "Rispondi in modo semplice, per favore. Una parola o una frase breve va bene."

If you need help in Italian:

Puoi ripetere, per favore?
Più piano, per favore.
Che cosa vuol dire…?
Come si dice ___ in italiano?

Can you repeat, please? · Slower, please. · What does … mean? · How do you say ___ in Italian?
